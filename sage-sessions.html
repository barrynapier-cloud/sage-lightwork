Tier 1 · EstablishedEvidence

40 Hz gamma stimulation drives real neurophysiology.

At MIT, the Tsai lab showed 40 Hz sensory flicker entrains cortical gamma and, in animal models, clears amyloid-beta by driving glymphatic flow, an effect specific to 40 Hz. The steady-state visual response to flicker is textbook, replicated neuroscience. This is the strongest ground in the field.

Nature 2016 · Tsai labNature 2024 · glymphaticJ. Vision 2015 · SSVEP
Tier 2 · EmergingEvidence

For mood, focus and anxiety, the trials are promising but mixed.

A 2024 double-blind RCT in Nature Scientific Reports (n=262) found a five-minute audio-visual session matched or beat twenty-two minutes of meditation on anxiety, depression and cognition. The honest caveats: several supporting studies are industry-funded and effect sizes vary. Promising is not the same as proven.

Nature Sci Reports 2024PLOS Mental Health 2026
Tier 3 · ExperientialEvidence

The "sober psychedelic" journey is remarkable to feel, and under-studied.

Stroboscopic light produces vivid altered states, documented since the 1950s and explored today by Anil Seth's team at Sussex. What doesn't yet exist is placebo-controlled evidence of durable change, some key work is still a preprint. We think it's extraordinary. We won't dress it up as a clinical outcome.

Dreamachine · SussexSchwartzman 2019 · preprint

The technology's own honest footnote. Cognito Therapeutics' Phase 2 trial of home 40 Hz stimulation missed its primary endpoint while hitting several secondary measures; Phase 3 is fully enrolled. Most marketing never mentions the miss. We do, because the people we want at Sage are the kind who check.

Safety, without spin

Powerful enough to work. Which is why we screen.

Some devices call themselves "totally safe." The science doesn't support that, so we won't say it. Flickering light affects a small number of people, and Sage's advantage over a device bought online is simple: a clinician screens you first.

The strongest-evidence frequency, 40 Hz, sits outside the highest-risk 15–25 Hz band, and even the browser preview here avoids it.

Before you book

The questions first-timers ask.

Is it safe?
For most people, yes. The main consideration is photosensitive epilepsy (about 1 in 4,000 people), which is exactly why every first visit begins with a clinical screening. If you have a seizure disorder, are pregnant, or have a serious neurological condition, tell us, we'll advise honestly and may recommend something else at Sage.
Will I actually feel something?
Almost everyone does, from the first session. With your eyes closed the light creates vivid colour and movement, and the shift, calmer, clearer, deeply relaxed, is usually obvious by the time you stand up. Depth grows with repeat sessions.
Is this like taking a psychedelic?
The Journey session can produce genuinely psychedelic-like visuals and altered states, sober, legal, and over in about twenty minutes. Be clear-eyed about the evidence: we place that experience in our most experiential tier. It's remarkable to feel; it is not a substitute for medical treatment.
What do I wear, and what do I bring?
Just wear something comfortable. You'll recline with your eyes closed and headphones on, nothing to bring or prepare. Arrive a few minutes early for your first visit so we can complete your screening.
Can I drive afterwards?
Yes. There's nothing in your system, the effect is your own nervous system settling, and it fades cleanly. We build in a few quiet minutes so you leave grounded and clear to drive.
How often should I come?
Once is a real experience on its own. For a lasting shift in sleep, stress or focus, most people do a short series, which is why the 5-pack and membership exist. Your practitioner will suggest a cadence.
